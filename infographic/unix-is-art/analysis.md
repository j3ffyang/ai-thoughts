## Main Topic

A personal essay on the Unix philosophy as an aesthetic — its history from 1969 to 2004, its core principles, its critics, and why it reads as art rather than method.

## Learning Objectives

After viewing this infographic, the viewer should understand:

1. What the Unix philosophy is — a folk tradition, not a specification — and its timeline (1969 → 2004).
2. The core principles (do one thing well, compose, simplicity, and the rest).
3. Why it is "art": the beauty is not in any single part but in how the parts fit.

## Key Data Points (Verbatim)

- "The Unix philosophy is not a specification."
- "the power of a system comes more from the relationships among programs than from the programs themselves"
- "write programs that do one thing and do it well; write programs to work together; write programs to handle text streams, because that is a universal interface."
- "Keep It Simple, Stupid"
- "the expertise that the Unix culture transmits"
- "adoring admirers have fed Linux goodies to a disheartening state of obesity"
- "the tools that Dennis built — and their direct descendants — run pretty much everything today."
- "if you had a microscope and could look in a computer, you'd see his work everywhere inside."
- Years: 1969–70, 1974, 1978, 1984, 1994, 2004; Oct 12, 2011.
